There is a kind of tool that looks impressive in demos and feels exhausting in daily use.

It usually has one or more of these symptoms: too many states, too many surface-level options, too much ceremony before you can do obvious work, and a persistent need to explain itself. The problem is not complexity by itself. Some tasks are genuinely complex. The problem is performative complexity.

I care about tools that reduce drag in the path I actually repeat.

For a writing system, that means:

- opening a draft quickly
- previewing it without waiting
- publishing it without hunting through settings
- trusting that the output will remain stable

For a frontend system, it means something similar:

- components are easy to find
- styles are consistent without becoming rigid
- build steps are boring
- debugging feels local instead of mystical

The reason I keep returning to simpler stacks is not nostalgia. It is throughput. When the tool gets out of the way, your standards can go up because your energy is not being spent on infrastructure theater.

That is also why I think personal websites deserve engineering restraint. You do not need enterprise machinery to publish a thoughtful essay. You need a setup that does not punish you for wanting to publish one more paragraph today.

The strongest compliment I can pay a tool is that it lets me stop thinking about the tool. Everything else is secondary.
